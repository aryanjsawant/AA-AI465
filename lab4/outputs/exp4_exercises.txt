Discussion: chunk_size=1 (no overlap) is the setting that breaks: the uniform-cost-search finality fact spans two adjacent sentences ('It replaces the FIFO queue with a priority queue...' / 'A node is only finalised when it is popped...'), so with one-sentence chunks and no overlap that fact is split across two separate chunks and the best matching single chunk falls to rank 4 instead of rank 1 - a direct demonstration that retrieval can only return what chunking has made retrievable. Every 2-sentence setting (overlap 0 or 1) keeps that same fact whole in one chunk and recovers rank 1 with a much higher score (0.50-0.52). Between the two, chunk_size=2/overlap=1 is the one to ship: overlap=0 scores marginally higher on this small corpus but relies on the sentence boundaries happening to line up with each fact; the 1-sentence overlap costs 4 extra chunks (13 vs 9) but guards against a fact that straddles a chunk boundary in a way overlap=0 cannot. The largest setting (4/2) does not miss any fact here because the corpus documents are short enough that a 4-sentence window still isolates one topic, but on a longer, more varied corpus its wider window would increasingly dilute the TF-IDF vector with unrelated sentences and push the right chunk down in rank - a risk not worth taking for the small score gain it shows on two of the four questions here.
