The score falls by 1 for every step after the world is clean:
the reflex agent oscillates A -> B -> A -> ... for ever.

            rules: Dirty -> Suck, Clean -> NoOp;  world: only B dirty

Performance   : score = 0

Step  | Percept          | Action | Resulting world state     | Reward  | Score  | CleanCredit

7     | (A, Clean)       | NoOp   | A=Clean  B=Dirty  agent@A |      +0 |     +0 | 7
8     | (A, Clean)       | NoOp   | A=Clean  B=Dirty  agent@A |      +0 |     +0 | 8
9     | (A, Clean)       | NoOp   | A=Clean  B=Dirty  agent@A |      +0 |     +0 | 9
10    | (A, Clean)       | NoOp   | A=Clean  B=Dirty  agent@A |      +0 |     +0 | 10

Final state   : A=Clean  B=Dirty  agent@A
Performance   : score = 0   (clean-square credit = 10 over 10 steps)

It stops paying the movement cost, but it never reaches B either:
B stays dirty for ever.  The percept (A, Clean) has to be mapped to
exactly one action, and the same percept occurs both when the world
is finished and when B is still dirty.  No memoryless rule table can
be right in both cases - the limitation is the architecture, not the
choice of rules.
